Do you need statistics?

  • A two question test (both must be YES)
    • In a deterministic world, do you know how to answer your question?
      • Is the question answerable in the real world?
      • How do you use a number to answer the scientific question?
    • In a world subject to variation and uncertainty, do you now how you would answer your question if you had measured the entire population?

Purpose of descriptive statistics

  • Detect errors in the data
    • Missing data, mis-coded values
  • Describe the materials and methods used

  • Assess conditions that might alter analysis methods
    • Modeling interactions (effect modification)
    • Adjusting for confounding
    • Adjusting for variables that provide additional precision

    • Multivariate data

How does correlation happen?

  • Question: Are observations intrinsically related to other observations?
    • Cluster or multi-level sampling
      • School or community based randomized interventions
      • Family studies
      • Complex survey samples
    • Repeated measures on the same subject
      • Longitudinal (over time) studies
    • Multivariate outcomes
      • \(FEV_1\) and \(FVC\) considered simultaneously as an outcome for pulmanry function
    • Contagion: One outcome may directly affect another
      • Infection: correlated in families ecause it is transmitted more easily
      • air pollution
    • Intrinsic correlation (non-random samples)
      • Time series

Benefits of longitudinal data analysis

  • Longitudinal study: subjects are usually measured repeatedly over time
  • Cross-section (independent data) study: a single outcome is measured for each subject
  • In longitudinal data analysis (LDA), we can investigate:
    • Temporal changes within individuals (age effects)
    • Differences between subjects in tehir baseline levels (cohort effects)
    • With independent data, we can only investigate cohort effects, ageing effects are not separately identifiable
  • LDA requires special statistical methods because observations tend to be correlated within individuals
    • Methods you have probably learned (until today) were for independent data

January 15

Why longitudinal data analysis methods?

  • The repeated observations on a subject, (\(Y_i1,, Y_i2, .., y_in\)) are very likely to be correlated
    • The assumption of independent observations will be violated
  • Using (independent data) regression techniques may have problems
    • Precision estimates (standard errors) will be biased
    • Mean parameters will often be inefficiently estimated
    • Inference (tests) will be incorrect
    • Correlation may be of scientific interest (and will be ignored)
  • Note: a sample unit or cluster is something like a family, a school, a hospital etc. that is a cluster of observations

Ignoring Correlation

  • Question: What are the effects?
    • On estimated means, rates (prevalence, incidence), regression coefficients or the precision of the estimates?
    • Estimated means: none. Why?
      • Let \(Y_{ij}\) be the outcome on the \(j_{th}\) unit in the \(i_{th}\) cluster. Let I be the total number of clusters. Assume all clusters have the same number of units (\(n_1 = n_2 = ... = n_I = n\)), all observations within a cluster have the same mean, \(E[Y_{ij}] - \mu_i\) and there is a common variance among all observations, \(V[Y_{ij}] = \sigma^2\).
      • Stuff happens. Turns out the estimators for \(\beta\) are unbiased.
    • Estimated variance (precision).
      • Math happens. Turns out that \(V[\bar{Y_i}] = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\times\left[1 + \rho(n - 1)\right]\). The right-hand side of this is the variance inflation factor.
