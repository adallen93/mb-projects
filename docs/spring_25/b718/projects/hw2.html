Crouse (Am J Cardiol, 2001) noted that intima-media thickness (IMT) of the carotid arteries is a generalized index of atherosclerotic disease. Previous investigators (Spence JD, Am J Cardiol 2002; Folsom AR, Stroke 1993; Howard G, Stroke 1993) have reported that IMT ranges between 0.5 to 1 mm in the general population and increases approximately 0.010 to 0.018 mm/year. Higher values of IMT are associated with more diseased arteries and cardiovascular events. These investigators and others have noted that lifestyle changes or medications may slow, arrest or induce regression in IMT. An investigation of an experimental statin – a medication for individuals at high risk of having cardiovascular disease events (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke) – was conducted to evaluate the effect of the drug on intima-media thickness (IMT) of the carotid arteries over a one-year period. In the prospective interventional study, approximately 2000 older patients (ages between 65-95 years) were randomized equally in a blinded fashion to receive either 40 mg of an experimental statin or a placebo. Treatments were administered daily for a year.

The intima-media thickness (IMT) of the common carotid arteries were measured by ultrasonography and were collected at two times for the study: prior to the drug or placebo administration (Year 0), and one year later (Year 1).

The data are provided in an Excel file imtdata.xlsx on the class website. The study variables are:

  • id: subject id number
  • trt: statin treatment (0=placebo, 1=40 mg experimental statin)
  • age: age of subject in years
  • imt0: IMT measurement at Year 0 (baseline)
  • imt1: IMT measurement at Year 1 (end of study)
  • imtdif: difference in one-year IMT measurements
  • male: male sex (0=female, 1=male)
  • smk: current smoking status (0=No, 1=Yes)

The dataset is formatted with one record per patient. There are no missing values.

Post Model

The post model is the model that only considers the outcome at timepoint t=1. The formula for this model is \(Y_{i1} = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_i + \epsilon_i\). Here are the coefficient interpretations: - Intercept: The intercept is the expected (or average) IMT measurement at timepoint 1 when ‘trt’ = 0, i.e. it is the mean IMT measurement for the placebo group at timepoint 1. This is not particularly interesting. The value of the point estimate is 1.05 and the 95% conficence is [1.036, 1.064] (p < 0.05). The p-value is not meaningful because we don’t care if the mean IMT for the placebo group is significantly different from 0 because it doesn’t make clinical sense for it to be 0. - trt: The ‘trt’ variable is the variable associated with the treatment group. The estimate -0.039 is the expected change in IMT between the treatment and control groups. Thus, we expect that on average, participants receiving statin have 0.039 units lower IMT measurements at timepoint 1 compared to participants in the control group. The 95% confidence interval is [-0.059, -0.02] (p < 0.05). This indicates that the change is significantly different than 0. This is an important result.

Change Model

The change model is the model that considers the outcome as the difference of timepoint 1 and timepoint 0 (i.e. change from baseline). The formula for this model is \((Y_{i1} Y_{i0}) = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_i + \epsilon_i\). Here are the coefficient interpretations: - Intercept: The intercept is the expected (or average) change in IMT measurement from baseline to timepoint 1 for the placebo group. This can be useful knowledge, as we may be interested to know if those in the placebo group are experiencing an increase in IMT measurements. The value of the point estimate is 0.01 and the 95% conficence is [0.0, 0.023] (p < 0.05). The first thing to note is that our point estimate is in the range provided in the accompanying literature, which is what we expect for those not receiving treatment. The lower bound is not exactly zero but it is close to zero. There is a significant increase in IMT over time for those not being treated with statin. - trt: The estimate -0.032 is the expected change in IMT baseline to t1 changes between the treatment and control groups. This estimate suggests we would expect the IMT change in a person treated with statin to be 0.032 mm lower than for a person not treated with statin. The 95% confidence interval is [-0.048, -0.017] (p < 0.05). This indicates that the change is significantly different than 0.

ANCOVA Model

The ANCOVA model is the model that considers the outcome as timepoint 1 while adjusting for timepoint 0 as a covariate. The formula for this model is \(Y_{i1} = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_i + \beta_2 Y_{i0} + \epsilon_i\). Here are the coefficient interpretations: - Intercept: The intercept is not really interpretable. It is the expected (or average) IMT measurement at timepoint 1 for the placebo group when the baseline value is 0. This is not interpretable because the baseline shouldn’t ever be equal to 0. The value of the point estimate is 0.319 and the 95% conficence is [0.279, 0.359] (p < 0.05). The p-value and confidence interval are not of much interest. - trt: This coefficient is the effect of statin on IMT at timepoint 1, adjusting for baseline measrements. The estimate -0.034 is the expected change in IMT between the treatment and control groups, adjusting for baseline. Thus, we expect that on average, participants receiving statin have 0.034 units lower IMT measurements at timepoint 1 compared to participants in the control group, holding baseline constant. The 95% confidence interval is [-0.049, -0.02] (p < 0.05). This indicates that the change is significantly different than 0. This is important.

Part b

Do any of the models provide valid estimates of a treatment effect? Please explain why or why not.

Yes, all three models provide valid estimates, but they reflect different aspects of the treatment effect. The post model is useful but doesn’t control for baseline differences, the change model offers a treatment effect on changes, and the ANCOVA model provides the most precise and useful estimate by controlling for baseline IMT levels. Each has its strengths, but I think the ANCOVA model is the most reliable for understanding the treatment’s effect while accounting for baseline measurements.
